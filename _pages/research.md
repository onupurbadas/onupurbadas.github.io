<p>
My research asks how <strong>power and structural inequality shape environmental and economic outcomes</strong>. I study what happens to forests and local economies when historically marginalized groups gain political power, and how environmental stressors such as heat and air pollution affect health and well-being.
</p>

<hr style="margin: 2em 0;">

<h4>Job Market Paper</h4>

<p><strong>Caste Reservations, Development, and Forest Cover in India</strong> (2026)</p>
<p>
  <a href="/assets/pdf/caste_development_and_forests_draft_onupurba_das.pdf" target="_blank"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> Paper</a>
  &nbsp;|&nbsp;
  <a href="/projects/3_project">Read more &rarr;</a>
</p>
<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>Leveraging the quasi-random rotation of chairperson reservations for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) across 5,033 village councils in Karnataka, India (1994&ndash;2010), I estimate the effects of marginalized communities' representation in leadership on satellite-measured nighttime lights and forest cover. SC/ST-led councils see higher local economic activity but lower forest cover, with larger effects under ST leadership, revealing trade-offs between development and conservation.</p>
</details>

<h4>Working Papers</h4>

<p><strong>Political Reservations for Women, Implementational Bias, and the Provision of Environmental Public Goods in Rural India</strong>, with <a href="https://people.umass.edu/jmullins/index.html" target="_blank">Jamie T. Mullins</a> (2026)</p>
<p>
  <a href="/assets/pdf/women_and_forests_draft_onupurba_das.pdf" target="_blank"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> Paper</a>
</p>
<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>This paper examines the impact of women's leadership in village councils on forest conservation in rural India. Using randomized reservations for women chairs and vice-chairs in Karnataka's village councils from 1992 to 2010, we analyze changes in forest cover using satellite data. Forest cover shows the greatest improvement when both positions are held by women over multiple election terms, emphasizing the importance of sustained leadership and multiple positions of power. However, such instances occur less frequently than expected under randomization, raising questions about structural and societal barriers to real power transfer.</p>
</details>

<p style="margin-top: 1.5em;"><strong>Temperature, Particulate Matter, and Mental Health in India</strong>, with <a href="https://people.umass.edu/jmullins/index.html" target="_blank">Jamie T. Mullins</a> and <a href="https://www.ashoka.edu.in/profile/kavita-sardana/" target="_blank">Kavita Sardana</a> (2024)</p>
<p>
  <a href="/assets/pdf/Temperature_PM_and_Mental_Health_in_India_onupurba_das.pdf" target="_blank"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> Paper</a>
</p>
<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>This paper investigates the link between exposure to high temperatures and particulate matter and self-reported mental health in India. Using data from a massive nationwide survey, we find that environmental stressors are predictive of poorer mental well-being. Interestingly, harms from particulate matter exposure are lower on hot days, suggesting an adaptive spillover effect.</p>
</details>
