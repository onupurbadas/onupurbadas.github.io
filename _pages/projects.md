<p> I'm an applied economist working at the intersection of environment, development and inequality. </p>

  <p>My current is guided by three central questions:</p>
  <ol>
  <li>How does the representation of historically marginalized groups in local institutions influence environmental outcomes?</li>
  <li>Under what conditions do policies aimed at redistributing power lead to substantive changes environmental outcomes?</li>
  <li>Reversing the lens: How do environmental stressors shape mental health and well-being?</li>
  </ol>

  <p> Broadly I care about how environmental, social, and economic systems interact to shape well-being and inequality. and how we as researchers and students interact and facilitate and stop these systems. And I am interested in exploring other topics like mental health impacts of environmental change, such as recurrent natural disasters or the loss of green spaces, and how communities adapt to these stressors. I’m also interested in how institutional norms shape political behavior, particularly the disconnect between scientific training and status quo-aligned worldviews. Relatedly, I plan to investigate how economics as a discipline self-selects for certain skills and dispositions, determining who gets hired, what gets rewarded and promoted, and ultimately, which questions are asked and which are left unexplored. Finally, I plan to examine how global consumerism, such as the fashion industry, drives environmental degradation and economic disparities, especially in regions whose resources sustain industries that rarely return benefits. </p>

  <p>Feel free to connect with me at <a href="mailto:odas@umass.edu">odas@umass.edu</a></p>
