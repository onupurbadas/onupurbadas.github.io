I’m an applied microeconomist studying how power and structural inequality shape environmental and economic outcomes. I received my Ph.D. from UMass Amherst in 2026, advised by Jamie T. Mullins, and am currently on the job market.

Using India’s randomized political reservations in village councils, I study what happens to forests and local economies when women and marginalized caste groups take office. I also study health and public policy: how rising temperatures and air pollution affect the mental health of older adults, and how hospitals can prevent pneumonia among patients. I combine satellite data, nighttime lights, GIS, administrative records, and large surveys.

Teaching

I build participatory, inclusive classrooms where students learn by solving problems together. I have taught several courses at UMass Amherst as instructor of record, from environmental economics to first-year seminars, and lead Stata workshops at UMass’s Institute for Social Science Research. As the Econometrics and Data Science Fellow at Amherst College, I advised senior honors thesis students. Across these settings, I teach economics as a human-centered discipline, connecting theory to the world students see around them.
