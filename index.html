When power shifts to historically excluded groups, does environmental governance move from extraction to conservation?

That question drives much of my work. I'm an economist studying environment, development, and inequality, with a Ph.D. from UMass Amherst (advised by Jamie T. Mullins). I'm on the 2026–27 job market.

What I've found so far

  • Marginalized leaders face a real trade-off. In rural India, village councils led by Scheduled Castes and Tribes see more economic activity but less forest cover. (job market paper)
  • Women's leadership needs support to last. Forest cover improves only when a woman chair is paired with a female vice-chair or follows a female chair's earlier term.
  • Heat and air pollution take a toll on mental health. I also work with public health researchers on hospital-acquired pneumonia in the U.S.

I answer these questions with satellite data, GIS, administrative records, and large surveys. I also teach economics through curiosity, empathy, and a critical lens, and I've worked with researchers and students at UMass's Institute of Social Science Research and Amherst College.
